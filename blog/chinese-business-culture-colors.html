Home Decor Industry Trends 2025: What U.S. Importers Need to Know

By Bonnie Woo September 13, 2026 5 min read

Sourcing is a technical exercise until it is not. Most stalled negotiations we see are not about price — they are about pacing, face and how a decision actually gets made on the other side of the table. A few cultural notes save weeks.

Colour Is Not Decoration — It Is Meaning

  • Red signals celebration, prosperity and good fortune. It is the default colour for packaging tied to Chinese New Year and for corporate gifting.
  • Gold implies prestige and wealth. Gold-and-red combinations read as premium in the domestic market.
  • White is associated with funerals and is generally avoided in gift packaging, even though it is a best-seller in Western interiors — a reminder that product colour decisions and packaging colour decisions follow different rules.
  • Green carries associations of health and freshness in some contexts but also of infidelity in others — avoid it as a standalone gift colour.
  • Black is neutral and modern in product design, but not a gift colour.

For export products this matters less in the product itself than in samples, gift boxes and factory-visit gifts. Sending white packaging as a New Year gift, for instance, lands badly.

Numbers and Symmetry

Six, eight and nine carry positive associations; four is avoided because it sounds like the word for death. Sets of eight or nine pieces photograph and sell better domestically, while Western buyers often want sets of four. If you are developing a range for both markets, agree on the piece count early — it changes tooling for trays and inserts.

Meetings and Decision-Making

  • Relationships first. The first meeting is usually about establishing trust, not closing terms. Expect a factory visit, a meal and questions about your market.
  • Face matters. Never correct a counterpart in front of their team. Raise issues privately, and always leave room for a revised offer.
  • The person who signs is not always in the room. Sales staff often relay terms to an owner who decides. Assume a two-step cycle and build in an extra week.
  • Silence is a negotiating tool. A pause after your price proposal is normal — do not fill it with concessions.
  • Written beats verbal. Confirm key terms by email after every call. This is what prevents the classic "I thought we agreed" gap once tooling starts.

Gift Etiquette, Briefly

  • Bring a small, branded, practical gift — a good pen, a notebook, something from your home region.
  • Avoid clocks (associated with time running out) and anything sharp.
  • Present and receive with both hands.

The single most useful habit: state your constraints plainly and early — target price, launch date, compliance standard — then ask what is possible. Directness with respect beats roundabout hints in every negotiation we have seen.

Where This Helps Most

Cultural fluency pays off most during development, not purchasing: sampling rounds, packaging decisions and the third or fourth revision are where relationships either carry you through or break down.
